# Prior research on Apple graphics and WindowServer compatibility

Research date: 2026-10-28. Shoji's current validation target is macOS 15.6 (24G84), Apple Silicon. Sources below describe their own work; none has been independently reproduced as part of this investigation. A project describing a feature or a paper proposing a method is not proof that it works on this host.

## Scope and conclusion

The question is whether a replacement compositor can support existing, unmodified macOS applications. That requires distinguishing (1) Apple GPU and display hardware interfaces, (2) the macOS WindowServer and application-facing protocols, and (3) rendering a different protocol such as Wayland on Apple hardware. Several projects cover substantial parts of (1) or (3). We found no demonstrated, general drop-in replacement for Apple's WindowServer protocol supporting unmodified macOS applications on macOS 15.6.

The closest direct WindowServer mapping found is security research on client stubs, Mach messages, and server handlers. Darling is a macOS application-compatibility effort, but its architecture is not a replacement WindowServer running on macOS. Wawona describes an experimental display takeover with a Wayland session, but its own validation records conflict with its higher-level claims and its Cocoa application bridge is explicitly planned. Asahi contributes exceptionally relevant reverse-engineering methods and hardware knowledge, not a complete WindowServer client protocol.

## Asahi: the hardware and firmware layer

Asahi runs Linux on Apple Silicon. Its work on the AGX GPU and display coprocessor (DCP) addresses hardware and firmware interfaces below the macOS window system. The DCP investigation describes m1n1 hardware tracing, mailbox endpoints, bidirectional re-entrant RPC, shared-memory arguments, a trace decoder, and a prototype implementation. That is strong precedent for a typed, evidence-backed protocol research workflow; it is not evidence that Asahi mapped AppKit-to-WindowServer behavior. [Asahi August 2021 progress report](https://asahilinux.org/2021/08/progress-report-august-2021/); [historical DCP tracer](https://github.com/AsahiLinux/m1n1/blob/c2c6da3df25c0605894244b4ea9387e882321efc/proxyclient/hv/trace_dcp.py); [DCP RPC decoder](https://github.com/AsahiLinux/m1n1/blob/c2c6da3df25c0605894244b4ea9387e882321efc/proxyclient/m1n1/fw/dcp/ipc.py).

For a separate GPU-side example, Mesa's macOS AGX wrapper records IOKit entry points and GPU submission memory. Alyssa Rosenzweig describes using small controlled graphics inputs and examining what changes. The transferable method is differential experiments, not the assumption that GPU command traces are WindowServer messages. [Mesa Asahi documentation](https://docs.mesa3d.org/drivers/asahi.html); [Dissecting the Apple M1 GPU, part I](https://alyssarosenzweig.ca/blog/asahi-gpu-part-1.html); [Tales of the M1 GPU](https://asahilinux.org/2022/11/tales-of-the-m1-gpu/).

Asahi reports firmware interfaces tied to particular macOS versions. This supports fingerprinting evidence by OS build and image identity, but its specific AGX/DCP firmware requirements must not be presented as WindowServer compatibility requirements. [Asahi Linux 7.2 progress report](https://asahilinux.org/2026/08/progress-report-7-2/).

Asahi's [copyright](https://asahilinux.org/copyright/) and [generative-AI](https://asahilinux.org/llm-policy/) policies govern contributions to that project. The latter broadly forbids material LLM contributions and specifically discusses use of LLMs to interpret m1n1 traces. This research reads their published material; it does not claim to follow their clean-room process or authorize AI-assisted contributions upstream.

## Direct WindowServer research

Chen, He, Grassi, and Fu's 2016 paper, [Subverting Apple Graphics](https://blackhat.com/docs/us-16/materials/us-16-Chen-Subverting-Apple-Graphics-Practical-Approaches-To-Remotely-Gaining-Root-wp.pdf), is a particularly useful starting point. Sections 3.1–3.4 describe historical CoreGraphics and QuartzCore client stubs, Mach IPC, message identifiers, server-side handlers, separate server loops and categories including windows, surfaces, regions and connections. Its target and security observations are from 2016: do not assume its prototypes, message IDs, rights checks, thread arrangement or vulnerabilities apply to 24G84. It does not demonstrate a replacement compositor.

The paper's historical move-window example also shows a connection/window ownership check and describes a separate QuartzCore service port. These are concrete reasons to compare owner, foreign and privileged contexts and to map more than the initial bootstrap endpoint; they are hypotheses to revalidate on 24G84, not proof of its current implementation.

[RET2 Systems' 2018 WindowServer investigation](https://blog.ret2.io/2018/07/25/pwn2own-2018-safari-sandbox/) reports instrumenting three historical MIG dispatch paths to identify handlers and inspect traffic. The approximately 600 handlers and x86-specific details are observations of its target, not inventory or offsets for our host. It did not implement a compatible server, but its dispatch-discovery method is a direct precedent for a build-specific, isolated tracing experiment.

Case, Moreira, Sellers and Richard's 2022 [memory-forensics paper](https://i.blackhat.com/USA-22/Wednesday/US-22-Case-New-Memory-Forensics-Techniques-to-Defeat-Device-Monitoring-Malware-wp.pdf) traces an event-tap client call through Mach messaging to a SkyLight server handler and correlates it with WindowServer state. This provides an example of joining client-side, transport and server-side evidence. It studies event taps and forensic detection, not the window-rendering protocol or a substitute server.

Quatermain's historical [Secret Sauce](https://alanquatermain.me/assets/papers/secret-sauce.pdf) describes application check-in, Mach IPC and injection-era interception. It is a method/history reference, not a current arm64e compatibility specification. [CGSInternal](https://github.com/NUIKit/CGSInternal) and [CGSPrivate.h](https://github.com/nocturneapp/nocturne/blob/master/CGSPrivate.h) are community inventories of candidate private interfaces; they are not Apple documentation, verified modern ABIs, or wire-protocol specifications.

The MOTIF authors' 2026 [private-framework signature-inference preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01673) combines metadata, binary analysis and candidate signature validation. Its reported benchmark concerns Objective-C method signatures on its own benchmark, not measured recovery of WindowServer's IPC protocol. Even a compiling header must be checked against independent behavior; Shoji's local transaction ABI investigation already shows why return-register guesses can be wrong. [Local transaction ABI notes](../../docs/TRANSACTION_ABI.md).

## Darling and compatible application frameworks

[Darling](https://github.com/darlinghq/darling) is a macOS compatibility project for Linux, including a Mach-O environment and reimplemented system components. Its [darlingserver architecture](https://github.com/darlinghq/darling-docs/blob/master/src/internals/darlingserver/README.md) is useful background for Mach-like services and lifetimes. It does not by itself replace Apple WindowServer on macOS while retaining Apple's existing frameworks.

Its maintainers' [GUI support discussion](https://github.com/darlinghq/darling/discussions/1563) gave a basic-apps-only assessment in February 2025. Later comments in the same thread describe progress in a separate fork, including limited reported operation of several Apple apps; those are contributor reports, not independent compatibility benchmarks or a claim about upstream parity. Track upstream and fork separately and verify exact revisions.

[GNUstep](https://www.gnustep.org/) and Cocotron-related AppKit implementations are useful examples of Cocoa-like abstractions and independent backends. They should not be described as implementations of Apple's modern private WindowServer wire protocol. [GNUstep backend architecture](https://mediawiki.gnustep.org/index.php/Backend).

## Wayland and display takeover on macOS

[XQuartz](https://github.com/XQuartz/xorg-server/blob/master/hw/xquartz/man/Xquartz.man) demonstrates integration of X11 applications with the macOS desktop. It is not evidence that native macOS apps can run without WindowServer.

[Wawona](https://github.com/Wawona/Wawona) hosts Wayland clients on macOS. Its ordinary mode runs within the Apple graphical environment. Its own [desktop-takeover documentation](https://wawona.io/docs/contributor/iland/) and [engineering notes](https://github.com/Wawona/Wawona/blob/development/docs/iland-mode-a-b-desktop.md) claim an experimental Classic mode that unloads WindowServer for a session and presents through an iland userspace DRM/KMS-shaped shim and framebuffer path. The project reports a kmscube own-display proof; it also describes launching Weston or Niri in that mode. We have not independently tested those claims or established compatibility with 24G84. The project says that mode needs full SIP disablement, does not render Aqua/Finder/Dock, and has unfinished restoration and multi-TTY milestones. It is display takeover, not evidence of an Apple WindowServer protocol replacement.

The evidence is inconsistent across time and documents: [issue 87](https://github.com/Wawona/Wawona/issues/87) and the [graphics epic](https://github.com/Wawona/Wawona/issues/122) describe earlier SIP-blocked or unverified live stages. Those issues may predate the current documentation; they make it necessary to identify a precise revision and reproduce the engage/restore cycle before relying on the claim. The original [CoreBedtime iland README](https://github.com/CoreBedtime/iland) separately claims Cocoa/AppKit use within Wayland but provides insufficient evidence there to establish compatibility or coverage. This is a lead for source and fixture investigation, not a validated solution.

Wawona's separate [Swinging Bridge](https://wawona.io/docs/user/swinging-bridge/) proposes making Cocoa applications appear as Wayland clients, including a privileged surface path. Its own documentation says both modes are planned and not yet implemented. This is directly relevant to follow, but is not an available solution for Shoji. The associated [2026 symposium poster](https://dc.ewu.edu/srcw_2026/works_2026/works_2026/15/) describes the broader Wayland-on-Apple research; it does not demonstrate WindowServer protocol compatibility.

### Follow-up: source and proof review

The original [CoreBedtime/iland `real` branch](https://github.com/CoreBedtime/iland/tree/real) uses a nested implementation in its inspected Weston path. Its [SDL backend](https://github.com/CoreBedtime/iland/blob/real/shims/sdl/src/sdl_backend.m) creates an SDL window and obtains an NSWindow, and its [launcher](https://github.com/CoreBedtime/iland/blob/real/run-weston.sh) runs Weston in that window. The [shim](https://github.com/CoreBedtime/iland/blob/real/shims/wayland-mac.c) intercepts Linux-style device operations and pumps SDL input. This is useful evidence for translating compositor interfaces onto macOS, but it relies on the macOS graphical environment for that path. The README's claim about Cocoa applications was not substantiated by the inspected bridge code or [test targets](https://github.com/CoreBedtime/iland/blob/real/test/CMakeLists.txt), which cover shims rather than unmodified native-app interoperability. This is not an exhaustive audit of every branch. The inspected root showed no repository-wide license; clarify rights before copying code, and check individual file licenses.

Wawona's [mode descriptions](https://wawona.io/docs/contributor/iland/) distinguish a nested mode from Classic: the latter is intended to unload WindowServer, use a DRM/KMS/GBM-shaped shim, and present through Metal and framebufferd. The project states that Classic requires SIP to be fully disabled and loses Aqua, Dock and Finder. However, its [Classic proof checklist](https://github.com/Wawona/Wawona/blob/development/docs/desktop-replacement-classic-proof.md) records a Weston/ANGLE test on macOS 26 build 25F80 with WindowServer retained, and leaves the 60-second takeover and logout recovery on HOLD after panics. Its higher-level claim of a kmscube own-display proof and [issue 87](https://github.com/Wawona/Wawona/issues/87), which describes Mode B desktop as planned, may refer to different revisions or milestones. Together they do not establish a reproducible, recovered takeover, Niri operation, or support on 24G84. The preceding account of takeover should be read with this qualification.

The [Swinging Bridge repository](https://github.com/Wawona/Wawona-Swinging-Bridge) describes ScreenCaptureKit and CGEvent for a native-app bridge, but explicitly says both its capture-based and privileged modes are planned. A compositor running Wayland apps inside macOS and a native-app bridge into Wayland are opposite integration directions. Neither the nested demonstration nor a successful display takeover proves that existing Chrome, Ghostty, or Finder windows work in Niri.

| Candidate | Evidence and result | Relevance to Shoji |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nested Wayland compositor | iland source runs Weston in an SDL/NSWindow; project demonstrations have not been independently reproduced here. | Useful research platform for Wayland applications; does not give Shoji control of native application surfaces. |
| Classic display takeover | Project claims and validation records conflict; sustained takeover and recovery remain unverified in the cited checklist. | Experimental separate desktop, without a demonstrated native Cocoa bridge. Restrict invasive work to a disposable guest. |
| Capture-based proxy of native windows | Architecture proposed by Swinging Bridge, not implemented there. Our prior standalone screenshot of an owned fixture succeeded; Shoji integration timed out. | Testable on the current host without replacing WindowServer, but capture latency, interactive input, occlusion and synchronization remain open. |
| Privileged control of original surfaces | No end-to-end solution demonstrated by these projects. Our ordinary-client foreign-clipping probe failed on its tested path. | Potentially closer to Niri's presentation model, but requires independent build-specific capability and recovery tests. |

### Recommended next experiments

1. Establish an isolated, bounded capture experiment using two disposable owned windows and a separate helper process. Measure frame cadence and end-to-end latency while windows are visible, overlapping, moving and resizing. Compare actual displayed frames and window identities; a single screenshot or native bounds readback does not prove smooth presentation. Test only fixture content, with a timeout and cleanup.
2. If the stream is adequate, build a disposable preview that composites captured frames under one animation clock and synchronizes a fixture focus indicator. Test click coordinate mapping, keyboard focus, resizing and transition back to the real windows; record failures and avoid presenting it as a production bridge until native input, occlusion, protected content and recovery are addressed.
3. Separately characterize privileged movement and clipping with owned and foreign disposable fixtures in an isolated guest. Establish observable output, input alignment, lifecycle and restoration; an API success code alone is not evidence of visible control. Do not infer the result of one privilege context from another.
4. Consider a nested Wayland compositor only if direct Niri behavior comparison is useful. Its working operation does not solve macOS native-app integration. Postpone WindowServer takeover or replacement until a disposable VM, reproducible recovery and an independent native-app compatibility path exist.

No third-party project was built or executed, no WindowServer or Dock service was restarted, and no system security setting was changed in this follow-up. The installed host tools observed include Xcode's `xcrun`, CMake, Meson and pkg-config; this does not establish that a third-party project or VM can be built or run successfully.

## Research plan derived from these sources

1. Keep a disposable macOS guest and recovery path for invasive tests. Do not infer that a host-compatible guest exists until the restore image and virtualization configuration are checked.
2. Identify the client-side stub, message transport, server-side handler and independent visible result for one tiny owned fixture operation at a time. Record build, architecture, binary UUID, permissions, request/reply descriptors and confidence.
3. Use differential tests for controlled changes in size, position, ordering and input, and check multiple clocks and asynchronous callbacks. Record port rights and resource lifetimes, not just byte strings. Separate screenshots/displayed frames from native bounds readback.
4. First build and test a small protocol recorder and isolated server for a client we control. Then attempt a minimal unmodified AppKit client in isolation. A successful custom client is not proof of compatibility with Apple clients.
5. In parallel, investigate a narrower privileged presentation capability: coordinated movement, clipping, input alignment and recovery of two foreign fixture windows. Compare Dock-based and WindowServer-side approaches separately. The existing ordinary-client clipping failure is not proof that all privileged methods fail, nor proof that they succeed.
6. Treat capture-based presentation as a separate fallback. A standalone ScreenCaptureKit fixture succeeded in prior investigation, while attempts inside Shoji timed out; neither proves smooth or correct interactive swiping.
7. Only consider whole-session replacement after tests cover application compatibility, graphics resource sharing, focus/input, display presentation, recovery and supported hardware. Track every macOS build separately and do not overwrite Apple's implementation on the user's sole working installation.

## Limitations

This is a literature and source survey, not a live validation, exhaustive catalog of unpublished work, legal opinion or assertion that replacement is feasible. Historical security papers document older systems. Project feature and compatibility claims are attributed to their authors; where evidence is documentation rather than a reproduced test, it is labeled accordingly. Existing concurrent project work was not altered.
